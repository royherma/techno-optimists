## Core idea

**A global community where people surface real-world challenges and technological ideas, then work together to investigate, build, test, and improve solutions.**

The important distinction:

**Technology is the tool, not necessarily the topic.**

The challenge could be:

* deeply personal
* local
* global
* serious
* weird
* experimental
* something merely annoying
* something nobody has attempted before

A family in rural Australia having a water issue belongs beside someone asking whether you could put GPU compute on autonomous drones.

What connects them is:

**Can technology help us understand, improve, or solve this?**

---

## The underlying thesis

We have reached a point where the cost and difficulty of **early-stage R&D has collapsed**.

Not literally zero. Hardware, manufacturing, field testing and expert knowledge still cost money.

But one motivated person can now use AI to do things that previously required a small team:

Research -> software -> electronics -> CAD -> simulations -> translation -> documentation -> visualizations -> data analysis -> sourcing -> iteration.

Then connect that person with thousands of other capable people around the world.

That creates something genuinely new:

> **Distributed applied R&D for everyday people.**

Someone finds a problem in Thailand.

Someone in California understands the physics.

Someone in Poland writes the software.

Someone in Shenzhen knows the components.

Someone in Bangkok builds version one.

The original person tests it.

All of that can happen publicly.

# What makes the product interesting

It cannot feel like a charity platform or an engineering Jira board.

The **problem hunting itself should be entertaining**.

You open the feed and encounter:

### 🐄 This farmer loses milk because of this cooling problem

[video]

### 🚁 Could compute move around a city using autonomous drones?

[concept image]

### 🦟 This village has this weird mosquito problem every rainy season

[photos]

### 🏠 My bedroom gets 8°C hotter than the rest of my apartment

[thermal image]

### 🐟 Fishermen here lose part of their catch before reaching shore

[30-sec video]

You immediately start thinking:

**"Wait... I wonder if you could..."**

That reaction is the product.

# The product object: Challenge

I would not force everything into "Problems."

Call the underlying object a **Challenge**.

A Challenge can begin as:

**Problem**
Something isn't working.

**Idea**
Something you think could exist or work differently.

**Experiment**
Something you're curious enough to test.

**Build**
Something you're already making.

Over time they all converge into the same lifecycle:

**Spot -> Understand -> Ideas -> Build -> Test -> Learn -> Improve**

And every Challenge becomes a living page rather than a disposable social post.

---

# The people layer

This is essential.

The platform isn't a database of clever ideas.

It's a network of **people who are good at making things better**.

Different people naturally play different roles:

**Scouts** find interesting problems.

**Thinkers** propose approaches.

**Researchers** investigate what's already known.

**Builders** make software/hardware.

**Experts** contribute specialist knowledge.

**Testers** are actually located where the problem exists.

Someone doesn't need to be an engineer to matter.

A farmer with the problem might be the most valuable person in the project because they can repeatedly test whether the solution works.

Profiles eventually become almost like a record of someone's practical intelligence:

> Roy
> Robotics · AI · Software
> 31 challenges contributed to
> 8 prototypes
> 4 successful solutions
> Thailand

That's much more interesting than follower count.

# The core social actions

Avoid generic **Like** as the primary mechanic.

I would experiment with:

**I have this problem**

**I want this**

**I have an idea**

**I can help**

**I'll test this**

**I'm building this**

**Follow progress**

Now engagement means something.

If 4,700 people click **"I have this problem"**, that's useful information.

If 34 electrical engineers click **"I can help"**, that's useful information.

# AI should quietly power the entire network

This should be deeply AI-native without becoming an "AI community."

Someone uploads a rambling video in Thai.

The system can automatically:

Transcribe -> translate -> identify the core problem -> extract constraints -> generate a clean Challenge page -> identify similar problems -> suggest useful questions -> find relevant research -> identify contributors who might know something.

When people start proposing solutions, AI can continuously maintain:

**What we know**

**What we've tried**

**Open questions**

**Current best hypothesis**

**Next experiment**

That massively reduces the coordination cost of strangers collaborating.

This is potentially one of the strongest parts of the concept.

# Why existing products don't quite do this

The pieces have been validated independently.

**Hackaday.io** has over a million members collaborating around hardware projects, but its center of gravity is projects people already decided to build.

**Zooniverse** demonstrates the enormous potential of distributed participation. More than 3 million volunteers across 160 countries have contributed to hundreds of research projects, recently surpassing one billion classifications. But those projects are generally structured by researchers and participants perform predefined tasks.

**FixMyStreet** lets ordinary people surface geographically specific real-world problems, add information and publicly track whether they were fixed. But the typical mechanism is reporting the issue to whoever is responsible, rather than crowdsourcing technological solutions.

That's why I think your interesting intersection is:

**Real problems + global people + technology + AI + experimentation + public progress.**

# What I would build first

Do **not** initially build GitHub + Reddit + Kickstarter + Discord.

I'd make the MVP almost stupidly simple.

### Home

Visual feed of interesting Challenges.

Photos/video first.

### Create

Three obvious choices:

**Share a problem**
**Share an idea**
**Share something you're building**

### Challenge page

Media
Problem/context
Location if relevant
Ideas
People helping
Experiments

**Help solve this**

Then ask:

> How can you help?

### Progress log

Every meaningful development gets added to the Challenge timeline.

That's enough.

# The main wedge

I would not initially sell:

> "Come collaborate on engineering projects."

Too much work.

I'd sell:

> **See interesting problems. Have ideas. Help figure them out.**

Browsing has to be enjoyable before contribution is required.

Think:

**TikTok curiosity + Reddit discussion + Hackaday building + global problem solving.**

Once the feed works, collaboration emerges from it.

# What I would deliberately avoid

**Don't make it startup-focused.**
A successful outcome does not need to become a company.

**Don't make it future-focused.**
Fixing someone's irrigation system this afternoon is equally techno-optimist.

**Don't make it charity-focused.**
Social impact should happen naturally without defining the entire culture.

**Don't require solutions.**
Finding and documenting a fascinating problem is itself valuable.

**Don't require expertise.**
The person experiencing the problem can be as important as the engineer.

**Don't become generic social media.**
Everything should eventually connect back to understanding, creating, testing or improving something.

# Potential moat

The long-term valuable thing isn't posts.

It's the accumulated graph:

**People <-> skills <-> problems <-> ideas <-> experiments <-> solutions**

Eventually Techno Optimists could know:

> These five people have successfully solved similar water problems.

> This challenge resembles seven previous projects.

> Three of those solutions failed because of humidity.

> This person lives 20 km away and owns the equipment required to test it.

That's when the network becomes powerful.

---

# Business model later

I wouldn't optimize for this initially, but there are obvious directions:

**Bounties:** fund solutions to Challenges.

**Sponsored Challenges:** companies, cities, NGOs or individuals pay to surface something they need solved.

**Talent:** organizations discover unusually capable builders by what they've actually contributed.

**Tools/components:** buy what's needed to reproduce successful solutions.

**R&D network:** organizations eventually pay the community to attack difficult problems.

The public community should come first.

# My recommendation

I would pursue this.

But I would **not** launch it as "a community for techno-optimism."

That's the identity and philosophy.

The actual product promise should be:

## **Find interesting real-world problems and help solve them.**

Then let **Techno Optimists** become the name for the people who do that.

That separation makes the brand much stronger:

**Brand:** Techno Optimists
**Activity:** finding, understanding and solving real problems
**Tool:** technology
**Network:** people everywhere
**Fuel:** curiosity
**Output:** experiments, prototypes and solutions

The biggest insight is that this isn't really an idea-sharing platform.

### **It's infrastructure for turning human curiosity into coordinated action.**
